---
title: What is team learning?
---

Team learning is a *collective process* in which a team creates, maintains, and uses knowledge to solve problems. This process is based on team members’ cognition and interactions. 

Team learning behaviors include sharing information, giving and seeking feedback, reflecting together, storing and retrieving knowledge, and engaging with resources outside the team. The frequency and quality of these activities vary over time, and what the team has learned becomes the basis for future learning. A team’s knowledge may be distributed across members, as in a shared mental model, or recorded externally, as in documentation.

A selection of team learning definitions published over the last three decades is provided below. 

::::{grid} 1
:::{card}
“An ongoing process of reflection and action characterized by asking questions, seeking feedback, experimenting, reflecting on results, and discussing errors or unexpected outcomes of actions” [p. 353, @Edmondson1999].
:::

:::{card}
“As a process, group learning involves the activities through which individuals acquire, share and combine knowledge through experience with one another [...] evidence that group learning has occurred includes changes in knowledge, either implicit or explicit, that occur as a result of such collaboration” [p. 370, @Argote2001].
:::

:::{card}
“The acquisition of knowledge, skills, and performance capabilities of an interdependent set of individuals through interaction and experience [...] a team-level property that captures the collective knowledge pool, potential synergies among team members, and unique contributions” [p. 86, @Kozlowski2006].
:::

:::{card}
“Compilation of team-level processes that circularly generate change or improvement for teams, team members, organisations, etc. [...] it consists of changing combinations of different types of processes [...] it dynamically translates a complex body of influences from multiple levels into different types of outputs at multiple levels, which in turn influence team learning” [p. 128, @Decuyper2010].
:::

:::{card}
“Team learning exists when all team members identify or interpret task-related knowledge from colleagues, explain their opinions based on their expertise with colleagues or synthesize different perspectives into better solutions” [p. 1432,  @Zhang2021].
:::
